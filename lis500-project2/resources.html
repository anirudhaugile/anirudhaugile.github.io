What is Implicit bias?

Implicit Bias refers to automatic associations or thinking that can influence how we interpret people and situations even when we do not consciously follow those associations. Project Implicit describes the Implicit Association Test, or IAT, as a way to examine attitudes and beliefs that people may be unwilling or unable to report directly.

What is intersectionality?

Intersectionality is a way of examining how different parts of identity and different systems of power interact. Race, gender, class, disability, nationality, and other identities do not operate independently in everyday life. Looking at only one category can hide important differences in how people experience institutions, policies, and technologies.

Our thoughts after the IAT

What stood out to us was the speed of the test. It asks people to make quick associations instead of simply stating what they believe. That made the experience useful as a prompt for thinking about automatic reactions, but we would not treat one test result as a complete judgment of a person's beliefs or character. The more useful question is what we do after noticing that automatic associations can exist.

Intersectionality makes that question more specific. Bias does not affect everyone in exactly the same way. A system may appear fair when looking at an overall average while producing very different outcomes for groups whose identities overlap in ways the designers did not examine.

Why this matters in technology

Software is created by people, trained or tested on data selected by people, and deployed inside institutions run by people. That means technical systems can reproduce patterns that already exist in society. Accuracy alone is not enough if errors are concentrated among particular communities. Developers should ask who is represented in the data, who is missing, who experiences failures, and who has the power to challenge a system when it causes harm.
