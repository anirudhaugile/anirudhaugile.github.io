Who we are

We are two students in LIS 500: Code and Power at UW–Madison. This site builds on our first project, where we reflected on our own identities and how they shape the way we experience technology.

Lathika Suresh

Computer Science, UW–Madison

I'm studying computer science, and I'm especially interested in AI, software, and product management. I like the technical side of building things, but I'm just as curious about why some products succeed and how people actually use them. AI is my favorite area right now because it went from something new to something almost everyone uses so quickly. Most of my CS classes focus on the code itself, so I wanted a class that looks at how technology affects people and society.

Taking the implicit bias test and reading about intersectionality made me realize that the people building a product don't always share the experiences of the people using it, and that gap, along with bias, can end up built right into technology like AI.

Anirudha Ugile

Computer Science, UW–Madison

I'm a CS student at UW–Madison. I'm mostly interested in how software actually gets built, and all the small decisions that go into it, like what data you use or who you test it on. In most of my classes, if the code runs and passes the tests, that's basically it. I never really thought about who it works well for and who it doesn't. I took this class because it seemed like a good way to look at the people side of tech instead of just the code.

The implicit bias readings made me realize that technology can be unfair even when nobody means for it to be, just because the people building it bring their own assumptions into it without noticing.

Why this project

Coming from technical backgrounds, we were taught to treat code as neutral. This class has pushed us to ask who designs technology, who it is tested on, and who gets left out. We built this site to share those questions with other students who are meeting ideas like implicit bias and intersectionality for the first time.
